How much capital does this FX & energy book need, and why?

CapitalLens replays more than 20 years of daily market moves through a trading book. It measures Value at Risk, Expected Shortfall and stressed VaR, back-tests the VaR model, and turns the results into a Basel 2.5 market-risk capital charge.       Method and data

      • Data: daily FX rates from the US Federal Reserve (H.10, via FRED) and Brent and Henry Hub prices from the US EIA, as republished by datahub.io. Only dates with all eight prices are used.
      • Historical simulation: today's book is revalued under each past day's relative price moves. Options are fully revalued, so their convexity is captured.
      • VaR is the 3rd-worst of 250 daily P&Ls (99%). ES averages the worst 2.5% (6.25 days). 10-day figures are 1-day × √10.
      • Stressed VaR uses the 250-day window in the data that gives this book its highest VaR.
      • Back-testing compares each of the last 250 days with the VaR forecast from the 250 days before it, then applies the Basel traffic light (green 0–4 exceptions, amber 5–9, red 10+), the Kupiec coverage test and the Christoffersen independence test.
      • Capital = max(VaR, m × 60-day average VaR) + max(sVaR, m × 60-day average sVaR), with m = 3 + the traffic-light plus factor. RWA = 12.5 × capital.
      • Simplifications: zero interest rates, constant implied vol, spot prices as a proxy for futures, a static book (positions don't change over the 60 days). An educational model, not affiliated with any bank.
